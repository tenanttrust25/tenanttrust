2. Are you "self-employed" for CRA?

For a sole proprietorship, the key test is business vs. hobby — and whether you expect to make a profit.

  • Tenant Trust as a sole proprietorship → this kit applies.
  • Tenant Trust incorporated → T2 corporate return + T4/T5 slips — the T2125 section doesn't apply; you need the corporate version instead.

Property management fees (managing others' properties, commission on rent collected) = business income on T2125. Rental income from properties you own = T776, not T2125.

3. The core form: T2125 — Statement of Business or Professional Activities

Net business income = Gross revenue − deductible expenses → flows to line 13500/13700/13900 of your T1, then CPP is calculated on it (Schedule 8).

Income to report

  • All gross revenue — before expenses — including fees/commissions, amounts billed but not yet collected (accrual method), and barter at fair market value.
  • GST/HST collected is not income — it's trust money (see §7).
  • Inventory applies if you sell goods; usually N/A for services.

Not deductible (classic mistakes)

  • Personal living expenses; personal vehicle commuting
  • Clothing (unless uniform with business logo)
  • Fines, penalties, illegal payments
  • 50% of meals/entertainment
  • Costs reimbursed by someone else
  • Club dues (golf memberships, etc.)

4. Business-use-of-home (line 8810)

Self-employed: no flat-rate option — detailed method only. (The 2020–2022 $5/day flat rate was for employees only.)

Eligibility: the workspace must be either (a) your principal place of business, or (b) used exclusively to earn business income and used regularly.

Deductible (proportion of): rent, property taxes, home insurance, utilities (heat, hydro, water), maintenance. If you own: mortgage interest (not principal). CCA on the workspace portion is possible but creates taxable capital gain on sale — usually not worth it for a home.

Formula: workspace area ÷ total finished area × eligible expenses. A shared space (desk in the living room) needs a reasonable time-and-space % — CRA scrutinizes this; document your method.

5. Motor vehicle expenses (line 8541) — the audit magnet

  • Method: actual expenses × (business km ÷ total km).
  • Track: opening/closing odometer, a contemporaneous logbook (date, destination, purpose, km) — reconstructed logs are weak evidence, and receipts for fuel, insurance, licence, repairs, lease or CCA + interest.
  • Pro-rate: fuel, maintenance, insurance, licence, lease (or CCA + loan interest), car washes.
  • Parking on business trips: 100% deductible, not pro-rated.
  • Commuting (home ↔ regular office) is personal. But home → client sites is deductible if home is your principal place of business.

Limits to verify: passenger vehicle CCA cost cap (~$38,000 area for 2025), lease cap (~$950/month), CRA per-km reasonable allowance rates (~72¢ first 5,000 km / 66¢ after — 2025).

7. CPP for self-employed (Schedule 8) + EI

  • 2025 YMPE: $71,300 (verify)  •  Second CPP ceiling: $81,200 (verify)
  • Rates: 5.95% base + 4% second tier — as self-employed you pay both shares → 11.9% base + 8% second tier on the excess.
  • Basic exemption: $3,500 (base tier). Rough max self-employed CPP 2025: ~$8,860 (verify exact published maximum).
  • EI: optional — only if you opted in for special benefits (maternity, sickness, etc.).
  • Instalments: net tax owing over $3,000 in 2024 and 2025 → quarterly instalments expected. Check CRA My Account.

8. GST/HST (13% in Ontario)

  • Small supplier: gross revenue under $30,000 over any 4 consecutive calendar quarters → no registration, no HST charged. (Verify — a rise to $50,000 was proposed in the 2025 federal budget.)
  • Over $30,000 → register, charge 13% HST, remit net of Input Tax Credits (ITCs) on business purchases.
  • HST collected is not your money — it's a liability; keep it separate in your books.
  • Unregistered and over threshold? CRA can assess HST on sales you never charged. Fix it before they find you.
  • Quick Method (if eligible): simplified remittance for service businesses.

9. Records you must keep (6 years from filing date)

  • All receipts, invoices, contracts
  • Bank & credit card statements — get a separate business account if you don't have one
  • Mileage logbook
  • GST/HST records
  • T-slips received (T4A, T5, T5013)
  • Digital copies are fine — CRA accepts scanned receipts

11. Pre-filing checklist

  • ☐ All 2025 revenue accounted for (incl. unpaid invoices — accrual)
  • ☐ Expenses categorized per master list (§3)
  • ☐ Business-use-of-home % calculated & documented
  • ☐ Vehicle log complete; business km % computed
  • ☐ GST/HST status confirmed (registered? Quick Method?)
  • ☐ CPP (Schedule 8) calculated on net income
  • ☐ CCA decision made (claim or skip deliberately)
  • ☐ 2026 instalments checked in CRA My Account
  • ☐ Any T4s issued for staff (deadline was Feb 28, 2026)
  • ☐ Filed via NETFILE or accountant; balance paid or payment arrangement set

12. If you're late (Aug 2026) — action plan

  1. File now — even a rough return beats another penalty month.
  2. Pay what you can; set up a payment arrangement (CRA online or 1-800-959-8281).
  3. Hardship? Request penalty relief (Taxpayer Relief Provisions, Form RC4288) — late-filing penalties get cancelled more often than people think.
  4. Don't ignore CRA letters — reply within their deadline.
