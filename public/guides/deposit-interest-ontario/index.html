Last month's rent deposit in Ontario

What you can legally collect, what you can't, and the interest you owe on it every year.

Updated October 2026 · Ontario Residential Tenancies Act, 2006

Short version: the only deposit allowed is for the last month's rent, capped at one month's rent. There's no such thing as a damage deposit in Ontario. And you owe the tenant interest on that deposit at the guideline rate, each year.

What you can collect

  • Last month's rent — up to one month's rent. Legal.
  • Key deposit — allowed, but must be refundable and no more than the actual replacement cost of the key.
  • Anything else — security, cleaning, pet, damage deposits — not allowed. A landlord can't collect them, and if one was taken, it can be ordered returned.

You owe interest — every year

A last month's rent deposit is the tenant's money. You owe interest on it at the rent-increase guideline rate, each year, for as long as you hold it. It's often paid as a credit toward rent.

Example: a $1,500 deposit held for a full year at the 2026 guideline of 2.1% earns $31.50.

How it's calculated (year by year)

Interest compounds by applying each year's guideline to the balance held:

Common mistakes

  • Collecting a "damage" or "pet" deposit — not permitted.
  • Holding the deposit and never crediting interest.
  • Charging a key deposit above the real replacement cost.
  • Raising rent on the deposit month without accounting for the interest credit.

Common questions

Can a landlord ask for a damage deposit in Ontario?

No. Only a last month's rent deposit (up to one month) and a refundable key deposit limited to replacement cost are permitted.

How much interest is owed on a last month's rent deposit?

Interest at the annual rent-increase guideline rate, accrued each year the deposit is held.

Does interest have to be paid out, or can it be a credit?

It's commonly credited toward rent, but it's the tenant's money — keep a clear record either way.
