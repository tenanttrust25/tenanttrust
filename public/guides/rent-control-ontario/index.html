Is my unit rent-controlled in Ontario?

One date decides it: November 15, 2018. Get this right and you know whether the guideline caps your rent — or whether you're exempt.

Updated October 2026 · Ontario Residential Tenancies Act, 2006

First occupied on or before Nov 15, 2018

Rent-controlled. Increases are capped by the guideline. Serve Form N1.

First occupied after Nov 15, 2018

Generally exempt. Any increase amount — but the 90-day and 12-month rules still apply. Serve Form N2.

What "first occupied" means

The test is the date the unit was first occupied for residential purposes — by anyone, not just your current tenant. A unit built and rented in 2019 is exempt even if your tenant moved in last year. A unit occupied in 2010 stays covered forever, no matter how many tenants come and go.

Examples

  • A 1970s triplex in Sudbury → covered. Guideline applies (N1).
  • A house bought in 2020 with a brand-new basement unit → the new unit is exempt (N2).
  • A purpose-built rental completed in 2021 → exempt (N2).
  • An older building with a unit added in 2019 → the added unit is generally exempt even though the building is old.

Who has to prove it

The landlord carries the burden of proving a unit is exempt. If you claim N2 and the tenant disputes it, the onus is on you to show the first-occupancy date. Keep your records — permits, occupancy, first lease.

What "exempt" does and doesn't mean

Exempt from the guideline is not exempt from everything. You still need to:

  • Serve at least 90 days before the increase.
  • Wait 12 months between increases.
  • Use Form N2 (not N1).

Common questions

Does rent control apply to condos in Ontario?

It depends on when the unit was first occupied for residential purposes — not on whether it's a condo. First occupied after Nov 15, 2018 → generally exempt.

If I renovate, does the unit become exempt?

Not automatically. Exemption turns on first occupancy after Nov 15, 2018, and on specific rules for additions and new units — not on a renovation. Get advice if it's a large project.

What form do I use if I'm not sure?

Use the rent-control test to sort N1 vs N2 — and confirm the unit's first-occupancy date, since it changes the answer.
